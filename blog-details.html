How to Choose Between Super 130s and Super 160s Wools

By Giovanni Rossi August 4, 2026 5 min read

When stepping into a luxury tailoring atelier, one of the first technical specifications presented is the 'Super' number printed along the fabric selvage. Many patrons assume that a higher number automatically equates to a superior suit. However, the reality of cloth performance is far more nuanced.

What Does the 'Super' Number Actually Mean?

The International Wool Textile Organisation (IWTO) defines the 'Super' grade based on the average micron diameter of the raw merino wool fibers before spinning into yarn:

  • Super 120s: Fiber fineness of 17.75 microns
  • Super 130s: Fiber fineness of 17.25 microns
  • Super 150s: Fiber fineness of 16.25 microns
  • Super 180s: Ultra-fine fiber of 14.75 microns
"For daily executive wear, a Super 130s weave provides the ideal equilibrium between buttery hand-feel and crease-resilient structural strength."

When to Choose Super 130s vs Super 160s

If you travel frequently, attend daily board meetings, or require a suit that withstands weekly wearing, Super 130s is our master cutter's top recommendation. It resists fraying at friction points like elbows and inner thighs while retaining a magnificent natural sheen.

Conversely, Super 160s or Super 180s wools offer unmatched lightness and silky drape, making them sublime for tuxedo galas, red carpet events, or summer wedding ceremonies where comfort and luxurious hand-feel take precedence over heavy workhorse durability.
